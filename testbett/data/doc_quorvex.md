# Quorvex Instruments: Company Profile

## Origins

Quorvex Instruments was founded in 1994 in the town of Brellin. Brellin is a quiet fishing town on the eastern shore of Lake Ostmar. The company was started by two friends, Talia Renko and Osric Penhallow, who had met at a winter market. Talia Renko had spent eleven years working as a glassblower before she turned to optics. Osric Penhallow had been a lighthouse engineer on the northern coast. Their first workshop was a rented boathouse with a leaking roof and a single wood stove. Their first product was a hand-polished lens for amateur astronomers. In the early years the two founders sold most of their lenses by mail, packing each one in straw and old newspaper. Local fishermen often stopped by the boathouse to watch the polishing machines, and several of them later became the company's first employees. In 1997 the company nearly ran out of money when a large order from a telescope dealer was cancelled. The founders kept the workshop open by repairing ship compasses and binoculars for the harbor, and a loan from a local savings cooperative finally allowed them to buy their first diamond-turning lathe.

## Growth and headquarters

The company grew slowly through the late 1990s, mostly by building custom optics for universities and weather services. In 2009, Quorvex moved its headquarters to the city of Vantor. The headquarters occupies the Weaver's Hall, a converted textile mill on the banks of the river Sell. The river Sell flows south into the Bay of Marrow. The renovation of the old mill took two years and kept the original brick chimneys, which now carry the ventilation ducts for the clean rooms. Today Quorvex employs 1,840 people across three sites. In 2023 the company reported revenue of 412 million euros. Quorvex has been listed on the Vantor Exchange since 2012. Its shares trade under the ticker symbol QVX. About a third of revenue comes from customers abroad, mainly port authorities, research institutes and environmental agencies. During the 2000s the company opened a small sales office in the coastal city of Merrin and began to exhibit at international trade fairs. Quorvex still builds almost all of its instruments itself and buys only electronic components from outside suppliers.

## Leadership

Mirela Szabo-Kent has been chief executive officer since 2017. Before becoming CEO, Mirela Szabo-Kent ran the Marine Division for six years. Mirela Szabo-Kent studied physics at Ardmoor Polytechnic. Ardmoor Polytechnic is located in the city of Calloway. The chief financial officer is Tomas Grieve, who joined the company from a shipping bank in 2015. The executive team meets every Monday morning in the old dye room of the Weaver's Hall, a habit started by Osric Penhallow. The board of directors meets four times a year, and employees elect two of its nine members.

## Divisions

Quorvex is organized into three divisions. The Optics Division is led by Benedikt Aaltonen. Benedikt Aaltonen was born in the mountain village of Arnholt. The Marine Division is led by Priya Castellane. Priya Castellane spent eight years aboard the research ship Albatross Grey before joining Quorvex. The Albatross Grey is based in the harbor of Tidewick. The Thermal Division is led by Jonas Wexley, the youngest division head in the company's history. Each division runs its own engineering team, but all three share the test facilities in Vantor, including a salt-spray chamber and a pressure tank that can simulate great ocean depths. The optics group is the oldest part of the company and grew out of the original lens workshop. The marine group was formed in 2005 to serve harbor authorities and survey ships, and the thermal group was created in 2013 after Quorvex bought a small infrared start-up.

## Products

The flagship product is the Lumen-4 spectrometer, launched in 2003. The Lumen-4 spectrometer was designed by Ilsa Varga. Ilsa Varga grew up in the fishing village of Kettleby. A Lumen-4 costs 18,500 euros. Every Lumen-4 carries a seven-year warranty. Ilsa Varga now directs the Fenwick Lab, the company's research laboratory in Vantor. Water utilities use the Lumen-4 to check the purity of drinking water, jewelers use it to grade gemstones, and lake authorities use it to monitor algae.

The Driftline sonar, launched in 2008, is used by research vessels to map the sea floor. The Driftline sonar has a maximum range of 6,200 meters. The Driftline sonar is built at the Grenholm factory. The Grenholm factory opened in 2006 and employs about 600 workers. Since 2012, the Northern Hydrographic Office has bought Driftline sonars under a contract for 300 units.

The Nightjar thermal camera, launched in 2015, can detect temperature differences as small as 0.02 degrees. The Nightjar is made by the Thermal Division. Nightjar cameras are assembled at the Ossery plant. Firefighters and mountain rescue teams use the Nightjar to find people in smoke and snow.

The newest product is the Kestrel-9 drone gimbal, released in 2021. The Kestrel-9 was developed by the Optics Division. It keeps a camera steady in winds of up to 70 kilometers per hour.

All Quorvex instruments are calibrated twice, once at the factory and once in Vantor, before they are shipped. Spare parts are kept in stock for at least fifteen years after a product is discontinued, and a repair team travels to customers who cannot send their instruments back.

## The 2011 fire

On 3 March 2011, a fire broke out at the Grenholm factory. Production at the factory stopped for fourteen weeks. Nobody was injured, because the night shift followed the evacuation drill that Osric Penhallow had introduced years earlier. The company kept paying all workers during the shutdown, a decision that is still remembered with pride in the town.

## Culture

The company motto is "We measure what others only guess." Every year on 12 October, employees celebrate Lantern Day. On Lantern Day, staff float paper lanterns on the river Sell. The company mascot is a grey heron named Pim. A sign above the press room at Grenholm reads: "No hand enters the press until the bell rings twice." In a letter to staff, Talia Renko wrote: "A small company can still keep a large promise." The quality pledge printed in every manual says: "Every instrument leaves Vantor tested in rain, salt and darkness." New employees spend their first week rotating through all three divisions. The canteen in the Weaver's Hall serves fish soup every Friday, a reminder of the company's beginnings as a workshop among fishermen.

## Foundation and competitors

The Quorvex Foundation, chaired by Odile Marchetti, pays for 40 university scholarships every year. Most scholarships go to students of engineering and marine biology. The company's main competitor is Calder & Voss, a sensor maker based in Pellmouth. Calder & Voss is larger than Quorvex, but it does not make thermal cameras. The two companies have competed for the same harbor contracts for more than twenty years, and engineers often move from one firm to the other.
